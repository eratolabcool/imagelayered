# Editing a Flat Poster Without the PSD

Campaign posters arrive as flattened exports. From a template tool, from a designer who left two jobs ago, from an AI generator — the story is always the same. The request that follows is also always the same: change the date, translate the headline, swap the product photo.

Without layers, that request means a rebuild. With decomposition, it's a revision.

## A poster is a predictable structure

Posters aren't arbitrary images. They're built from the same handful of parts: a headline, a logo, some body copy, a background plate, decorative graphics. When you decompose a poster, you get those parts back as layers, roughly in that shape.

This is what makes poster work the easiest case for layer editing — the structure the original designer imposed on the file is the structure the model recovers from it.

In the [editor](/qwenimagelayered), decompose at six to eight layers for a typical poster. Fewer and the copy merges with the art; you want text separated enough that each block is addressable on its own.

## The three most common edits

**Rewrite the text.** Select the text layer, describe the new wording. Placement, scale, and the layout around it hold still. This is the single most requested poster edit in existence — dates, prices, headlines, market names — and it's the one this workflow most obviously wins. If your text needs full re-typesetting rather than rewording, [separating the text properly](/blog/separate-text-from-an-image) is the deeper dive.

**Swap the product.** The product layer gets replaced; the background, shadow, and copy layers don't know it happened. Keep the shadow layer visible under the new product — a swapped product with no grounding shadow reads as pasted-on instantly.

**Restyle the background.** Same layout, different season. The headline and product stay put while the plate behind them changes mood. This is where campaign variants come from: one master poster, four seasonal backgrounds, and the version history to prove nothing else moved.

## Version snapshots before you experiment

The editor creates a version snapshot when you replace the source image, and you can snapshot manually before riskier moves. Use that. Poster revisions are 90% "same but different" requests, and the safety net that matters is being able to show exactly what changed since the approved version — and roll back if the answer is "too much."

Lock the logo and any legal-fixed elements while you're at it. Locked layers are carried into AI edits as preserve rules, which is how you keep the things that must not move from moving.

## The part nobody advertises

Decomposition on heavily stylized posters — dense illustration, baked texture, text dissolved into the art — is imperfect. You will get layers, and some of their edges will be approximate. The professional move isn't to demand perfection from the split; it's to edit at the layer level (rewrite the whole text block, replace the whole product) rather than trying to surgically patch an imperfect mask.

Fast poster revision isn't about recovering the designer's original file. It's about getting *a* working structure that's good enough to aim edits at. Aim at whole layers and the imperfections stop mattering.
