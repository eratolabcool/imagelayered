# Exporting Layers to Photoshop and Figma

The point of splitting an image into layers is not to live in one tool forever. You'll decompose in the browser and finish somewhere else — Photoshop for retouching, Figma for layout, Canva for the social resize, a marketplace listing that needs the product on pure white.

So the export story matters more than people give it credit for when they're comparing tools.

## What export gives you

Two paths, both in the [editor](/qwenimagelayered):

**Individual layers as transparent PNGs.** Select any layer, download it. What you get is that element on transparency, at source resolution, positioned nowhere — a clean asset ready to drop into anything. This is the one that matters for cross-tool work.

**The full composite as PNG.** Every visible layer rendered together, exactly as you see them on the canvas — positions, stacking, opacity, blend modes all applied. Use it when the image is finished and you just need the result.

One detail worth knowing: composite export runs locally in your browser. It doesn't consume credits. Decomposition is the paid step; getting your result out is not, at any resolution the editor supports.

## The Photoshop round-trip

Photoshop opens transparent PNGs natively, so the workflow is short: download the layers you need, drop them into a PSD, and they arrive as separate layers in the right order — select them all, and drag them into an open document.

Where this earns its keep is precision work. Decomposition gives you structure in seconds; Photoshop gives you pixel-level control over the seams. The combination is stronger than either alone: you skip the hour of manual masking that would have created those layers, and keep the tools you'd have used to refine them anyway.

One habit: export every layer you might touch, not just the obvious ones. The background you didn't plan to edit is the one you'll want when the client asks for "just a small change" to it, and re-running decomposition to recover it costs credits the original run didn't.

## The Figma / Canva direction

Same asset, different job. A separated subject layer dropped into Figma becomes a design component — reusable across frames with the layout rebuilt around it. In Canva, layers slot into templates for the social-size variants that every campaign eventually needs.

The pattern to internalize: decomposition output is *generic* material. Any tool that accepts a PNG accepts your layers, which means the browser editor is a front door, not a destination.

## What doesn't survive the trip

Be aware of what a PNG layer can't carry: no editable text, no adjustment layers, no effects stacks. A text layer exports as rasterized type — fine for composition, not for re-typesetting. If you need the wording changed, change it [in the editor](/blog/edit-a-flat-poster-without-the-psd) where the text is still an addressable layer, then export.

That's the practical division of labor: semantic edits where the structure lives, pixel edits where the precision lives. The PNG in between is the handshake.
